# Lifestyle action and recovery reference

AutoMindful targets Project Zomboid Build 42 and Lifestyle: Hobbies with the hidden Yoga skill. The traced official Workshop item is [Lifestyle: Hobbies](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3403870858), mod ID `LifestyleHobbies`, installed version `0.4.0.3`. Source paths below are relative to Lifestyle's `common/media/lua/`. These files remain in the user's Workshop installation and are not copied into AutoMindful.

## Native entry points

`client/ISMeditation/ZenWellnessContextMenu.lua` owns the active meditation and yoga menus. `ZenWellnessContextMenu.Options.Meditation.onAction({}, option, player)` and its Yoga equivalent select the current skill, animations, duration, bonuses, and sandbox options. Their `canPerform` functions report current gates. `ZenWellnessContextMenu.isValid` rejects death, a busy action queue, a vehicle, sleep, or furniture seating. Meditation requires sitting on the ground. Yoga requires standing.

The older `MeditateContextMenu.lua`, `YogaContextMenu.lua`, and `YogaSkill.lua` are deprecated. They do not define the active yoga progression. The short client timed-action files are also deprecated. The actual implementations live in `shared/TimedActions/LSMeditateAction.lua` and `shared/TimedActions/LSYogaAction.lua`.

AutoMindful calls the active menu entry points rather than reproducing their level tables or constructing replacement training actions. It uses the same upstream bag removal and heavy-item handling as manual training. Bags remain in your inventory after unequipping. Restore equipment yourself after the routine. Meditation has no native endurance gate, so AutoMindful does not wait for stamina recovery before starting it or interrupt it solely for low stamina.

## Meditation

Meditation is `Perks.Meditation`. The context menu rejects boredom above 30. During the action, normal stat updates increase boredom and neck pain at low skill. Boredom above 10 raises aversion, which reduces XP and worsens boredom or pain. Low boredom, skill, the Disciplined trait, higher meditation focus, mindfulness, being taught, and Yoga's Zen effect influence upstream results. Boredom is a menu gate rather than an automatic stop check inside `LSMeditateAction:update`.

The native action stops when the player aims, enters a vehicle, moves, runs, or receives `IsMeditationDisturbed`. `client/LSIsListeningEffects.lua` can mark meditation disturbed and enqueue `ToneDeafSuffering` when disliked music interrupts a player without Disciplined. The native action clears its disturbed flag during stop. Meditation stops restore held items and stop its sound. Its periodic stat updates award Meditation XP through Lifestyle's existing command handler, and normal completion awards a final update.

AutoMindful can take a useful care break at boredom 20, before the menu's hard gate. If no eligible literature is available, it continues while native meditation remains permitted through boredom 30. Only Train both permits yoga as boredom recovery; meditation-only stops at the blocker instead of training another skill. It resumes meditation through the same upstream entry point after reading. Reading never substitutes for meditation XP. Inventory transfers can pause and resume the routine; clear movement, sustained aiming, another gameplay takeover, or persistent disturbances stop it with a reason.

## Yoga

Yoga is a hidden skill, not `Perks.Yoga`. `client/Helper/HSMng.lua` owns `HiddenSkills.getSkill`, `getLevel`, and progression. The current skill tuple is `{level, currentXP, XPForNextLevel}`. Thresholds for levels 0 through 9 are 100, 250, 500, 750, 1000, 1250, 1750, 3000, 4000, and 9000 XP. Upstream requires XP to exceed a threshold to level up and carries the overflow. AutoMindful reads this state and never writes it.

The yoga menu checks `SandboxVars.Yoga.RequiresMat`, `Exhaustion`, and `Embarrassment`. A required mat must already be deployed under the player's square as the complete matching two-tile native yoga mat. Carrying a rolled-up mat does not satisfy the native check. The exhaustion setting can disable its gate or require endurance above 0.8, 0.6, or 0.3. The embarrassment setting can disable its gate or block at moodle level 1, 2, or 3. AutoMindful calls `canPerform` to honor those choices.

Each completed pose awards XP and normally reduces boredom by 5, pain, stress, and stiffness. A random pose failure can end the session with embarrassment and no XP for that pose. Normal completion can award bonus XP and Zen. The next session works through the native action again at the new level. Stopping early can remove earned Yoga XP and some Zen. AutoMindful normally lets yoga finish, even when a selected skill reaches level 10 mid-session. An urgent survival need or explicit cancellation can still interrupt it and incur the normal penalty.

Yoga's embarrassment fades through the existing `client/LSEffects/LSPerMinute.lua` updates. AutoMindful waits for that recovery if it blocks yoga, and meditates while yoga is blocked when the selected mode and boredom allow it. Its stamina recovery policy applies only to yoga: below 85% it waits for 95% before another yoga session. In Both mode, permitted meditation can run during that recovery instead of idling. If boredom also blocks meditation and no literature is usable, Both mode can wait for yoga recovery. It does not reset embarrassment, make poses succeed, skip cooldowns, change multipliers, or reduce XP thresholds.

## Survival and supplies

The installed game's `shared/TimedActions/ISReadABook.lua`, `ISEatFoodAction.lua`, `ISDrinkFluidAction.lua`, `ISWaitWhileGettingUp.lua`, and native inventory transfer utility supply the existing client actions. AutoMindful runs one batch at a time and uses their ordinary inventory, duration, animation, item-consumption, and multiplayer behavior. Portable hydration uses the current fluid-consumption action because the older `ISDrinkFromBottle` path refers to a missing thirst packet constant in the tested Build 42.22.2. That action's native fullness restriction still applies. Direct world-water drinking uses native `ISTakeWaterAction`, which has different eligibility and does not impose that fullness gate.

Useful literature has a negative boredom change and is not a skill-training book or an Uninteresting item. Illiterate characters can use picture books and pictures that native reading permits. `IsoGameCharacter.getReadLiterature()` records each named title's last-read night, and `isLiteratureRead` applies the native sandbox cooldown. AutoMindful prefers unread titles, then eligible rereads from oldest to newest. Recently read titles and permanently read print media are excluded while native reading cannot provide a benefit. Unnamed literature gets a local ordering record; it does not bypass native restrictions. Native print media may open its normal reading window.

Food must be unfrozen, unburnt, unrotten, untainted, nonpoisonous, and nonalcoholic. Dangerous raw food is excluded. Supported sealed items and cans use their native `OpeningRecipe`, `HandcraftLogic`, and handcraft actions with available tools. Output types and projected effects come from native recipe data rather than a copied can table; the actual opened item is inspected again before consumption. Surface-dependent or ambiguous opening recipes require manual preparation. Current hunger and thirst determine the requested portion; no extra calorie reserve forces another meal. Native food percentages refer to original base hunger, so AutoMindful converts the requested remaining-food fraction before using the normal eat action. Calories do not rank supplies. Signed effects account for worsening the other need, and normal boredom effects still apply.

Portable drinks use their current native fluid properties. Safe milk and other nourishing or hydrating fluids can restore the needs their properties support. Tainted, poisonous, alcoholic, or sickness-producing sources are rejected. Ordinary portable drinking follows the native UI capacity limit, with worn hydration containers supported separately. Nearby sinks, baths, toilets, showers, and pure-water ground containers use native `ISTakeWaterAction` without picking up the source. Native plumbing, remaining fluid, water shutoff, reach, locks, safehouses, and taint remain authoritative. The native action takes only the water needed for current thirst. Large ground sources do not need to be carried. Non-water fluids continue through portable or worn item consumption.

Optional nearby collection uses the game's current adjacent loot containers, on the same floor and within one square. The native synthetic ground inventory has no container square, so dropped supplies use each world item's actual square. Nearby portable bags also lack a source grid; their containing item's world object supplies the native location. Native loot discovery still owns reachability. AutoMindful checks inventory room and transfers only the next needed item. It does not walk, open doors, search a building, deploy mats, empty other containers, or collect from inaccessible distant storage. Supplies inside carried bags are available without opting into nearby collection. Supply inspection reports usable items in inventory and optional adjacent loot separately.

Borrowed items retain their original container or floor position. Returned items in carried bags are resolved through native recursive item-ID lookup and treated as complete once they are back in that source. Known recipe tools, unused portions, and identifiable replacement containers use ordinary transfer or drop actions. If the original source is inaccessible, full, or rejects an item, it remains in inventory with a notice. Ambiguous or delayed replacement containers are kept rather than assigned to an unrelated source.

Food and hydration reserves are not a start gate. The routine uses optional care when supplies exist, continues through mild unmet needs, and stops for missing food or drink only when the corresponding native need becomes critical. The window is a view of the controller, so hiding it never cancels the loop.

When ordinary fatigue reaches 0.6, AutoMindful uses `ISWorldObjectContextMenu.onSleepWalkToComplete` to sleep on the current floor. That native function checks nearby zombies, pain, and panic and performs the game's ordinary sleep handling. AutoMindful also respects the normal one-hour sleep cooldown. It waits through sleep and resumes after waking. On servers with `SleepNeeded=false`, it does not invent a fatigue requirement. A server that requires sleep but disables it produces an explicit stop. A floor sleep is less comfortable than a bed and can cause the game's normal effects. A pillow on the training square or in hand improves the native floor-sleep category.

AutoMindful stops for movement, aiming, entering a vehicle, dragging a corpse, death, fire, bleeding, nearby visible or chasing zombies, health at or below 50, missing essential supplies, or repeated failed actions. It does not heal injuries, fight, smoke, take medicine, manage Lifestyle hygiene or toilets, or override sleep restrictions. Severe pain and panic can require recovery before sleep. A safe, quiet, lit training spot and appropriate provisions support long unattended progress. Ordinary world threats can still make training unsafe.

## Client and multiplayer behavior

AutoMindful contains client Lua only. Lifestyle must work normally in the session. Its own native actions still own XP, hidden-skill synchronization, mood changes, animations, and any commands sent to the server. AutoMindful adds no server command, XP grant, fake item, or checksum bypass. Build 42.22 normally reloads the server's advertised code-mod list during connection, so ordinary native multiplayer loading requires the server to list AutoMindful. Listing the mod does not add executable server code. The server can also reject the underlying actions according to its configuration. Client-only packaging does not override the server's authority.

Selected skill goals are level 10. Both mode alternates training when useful and uses yoga's boredom and pain recovery while Meditation remains unfinished. A single-skill mode trains only its selected skill and stops if care cannot resolve a blocker. Missing mats or a zero upstream XP multiplier cannot be repaired by automation. The routine reports blockers and stops after six accumulated game hours spent training with no observed XP change, across repeated sessions. Care breaks do not count as training time.
